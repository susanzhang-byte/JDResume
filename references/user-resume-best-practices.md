# Resume Best Practices — User-Supplied Standards (V1)

Source: user-attached **Resume best pratices.txt**, seven numbered sections. These are the owner's editorial defaults for JDResume V1, not independently validated ATS vendor rules. Apply alongside `SKILL.md`'s evidence preservation, original-format fidelity, and human-review requirements. A conflict must be explained, not silently resolved by deleting information.

## 1. Resume Writing Tips — Strategic Principles for Maximum Impact
- Treat the résumé as a strategic marketing document with an interview objective. Focus on compelling selling points rather than narrating an entire life story.
- Default to **one page**, prioritizing relevant experiences from the **last five years**, **when compatible with the candidate's seniority, original résumé, and explicit preferences**. Earlier relevant experience is not automatically removed; seek approval for substantive cuts and preserve source career history.
- Present strengths confidently, without inventing or exaggerating.
- Default to a clean **black-and-white**, main-body layout; avoid colors, sidebars, and graphics unless the original user-approved format is appropriate and ATS-readable.
- Maintain consistent formatting (source suggests **Times New Roman or Arial**), fonts, bullets and spacing.
- Address gaps or potential questions candidly; the source advises briefly explaining an employment gap in a **cover letter**, not hiding or fabricating it on a résumé.
- Include only claims the applicant can discuss confidently in an interview.
- LinkedIn is a conversational networking profile, distinct from the formal résumé; avoid direct copy-paste.

## 2. Understand Your Resume Audiences — Optimizing for Both Systems and Humans
- Two audiences: **ATS** and **people**. Use standard headings (Education, Experience, Skills), relevant *true* keywords and simple bullets; avoid decorative tables, graphs, images and headshots for default ATS layout.
- Recruiters look for relevance to the job description; hiring managers consider present ability and future growth and leadership.
- Maintain a comprehensive **master résumé / source-data inventory**, then create a JD-specific tailored version. The tailored one-page draft is a selective presentation, not a license to erase history from original documents.
- **Accuracy nuance:** the attachment asserts ATS can reject complex designs, but ATS behavior varies. Do not promise automatic rejection, universal keyword-matching or guaranteed parsing.

## 3. Resume Basics — Education
- Once the candidate has a college degree, usually include **Bachelor's degree and higher**, not high school (exceptions require applicant preference or role relevance).
- Spell out university and degree names rather than relying on abbreviations; source says write **“Master”** rather than **“Master's”** in full degree names (e.g., Master of Business Administration).
- Give graduation **month and year**, not a full day/date; right-align dates in the established source format.
- Education bullets must carry descriptive titles: **Honors:**, **Relevant Coursework:**, **Leadership:**, **Activities:** as appropriate, only if sourced.

## 4. Resume Basics — Experience
- Source preference: start accomplishment bullets with strong **past-tense action verbs** (Managed, Developed, Increased). For continuing work, avoid misrepresenting tense; present-tense is permissible for an active current duty when factually clearer.
- For freelance/consulting work, generally list the name of the applicant's actual firm as employer, then identify supported clients inside bullets; never invent a firm or client.
- Distinctly different duties require separate descriptions/bullets.
- Similar responsibilities under one company can use **grouped titles** and shared bullets, showing progression accurately.
- Dates, positions and companies must remain clear and consistent, with dates aligned far right when the source style uses that approach.
- Quantify results where genuinely provided, preserving original figures and units.

## 5. Resume Basics — STAR Model — Results-Driven Bullets
Use the attachment's four parts: **Situation, Task, Action, Result**. Translate these into concise résumé bullets: **strong verb + specific personal action/method/tool + scope + measurable or qualitative outcome**. Avoid forcing all four headings into the output text.

Attachment's illustrative before/after:
- Weak: “Worked on a marketing campaign with the team.”
- Strong illustrative rewrite: “Developed and executed a multi-channel marketing campaign using Google Ads and LinkedIn, increasing customer acquisition by 15% and reducing cost per lead by 10% within two months.”
**These metrics and tools are teaching examples, not facts about any candidate.** Reuse only as fictional illustrations, never as source evidence for a real applicant.

## 6. Resume Basics — Qualitative Impact — When Numbers Aren't Available
When there are no reliable numbers, show *evidenced*:
- leadership **recognition or selection**;
- **adoption** by other teams, scaling, or use as standard practice;
- **rapid progression** and promotions, if timing is documented;
- **foundational contributions** such as creating a framework, template, or process.
The attachment's illustrative example describes a Managing Director selecting a candidate after eight months to create marketing operations and valuation/data templates adopted by other teams. **Never transplant this into applicant history.**
Use qualitative specificity, not fabricated percentages or generic achievement claims.

## 7. Resume Basics — Skills & Interests
- Source proposes listing relevant qualifications beyond Education/Experience under labeled bullets: **Languages; Volunteer; Certifications; Community Service; Leadership; Software; Honors/Awards; Interests**. Select only applicable, genuinely reported categories.
- Source states the Skills & Interests section should have **at least one bullet**; implement **when actual content exists**. If there are no supportable skills/interests, don't invent filler; omit the empty section and flag missing information.
- Software/tools: state specific advanced capabilities (e.g., pivot tables, VBA, Power Query) **only if verified as applicant-reported**; no inferred proficiency.
- Language: show a stated proficiency label (e.g., professional working proficiency), and **ask or omit the label** when not supplied.
- Genuine professional-appropriate interests can be included if they strengthen the specific application; never invent them.
- Keep **AI/Technical**, **Professional/Other**, and **Language** entries separate according to existing JDResume standards.

## Conflict hierarchy / implementation
1. Factual accuracy, source preservation, applicant approval, and no fabrication are non-negotiable.
2. Explicit applicant preferences and approved source-format fidelity take precedence over generic stylistic recommendations.
3. Apply attachment preferences where compatible; note any departures in change log.
4. One-page and five-year recency are **prioritization guidelines**, not permission to delete an earlier role, membership, metric, original achievement or educational credential without approval.
5. Never treat uninspected applicant-reported metrics as independently verified; preserve them in the draft and list verification needs separately. Resolve apparent contradictions with the applicant before changing figures.
6. Skill outputs remain educational drafts for mandatory human review; no ATS score or interview guarantee.
